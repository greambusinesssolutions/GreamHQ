Buying software or AI for your business is easier when you know what to ask. A good provider will answer these six questions clearly and in writing. If they can’t, that tells you something too.

## 1. Which jobs will it do, exactly?

“Automate your customer service” is not an answer. Look for specific jobs: *reply to enquiries about delivery days*, *send a reminder two days after a quote*. If the scope is vague, the result will be too.

## 2. What does a person still approve?

Anything involving prices, discounts, refunds, complaints or promises should stay with your team. Ask the provider to show you exactly where a person steps in.

## 3. Where does our data go, and who owns it?

Your customer list, messages and payment history are valuable. Ask where they’re stored, who can access them, and whether you can take everything with you if you leave. The answer should be: you own it.

## 4. How will we measure if it works?

Agree on a few measures before you start: replies sent the same day, follow-ups missed, time spent chasing payments. Note today’s numbers so you can compare honestly later.

## 5. What happens when it gets something wrong?

It will, sometimes. Ask how mistakes are spotted, who is told, and how quickly they’re fixed. A good answer includes a person checking the work, especially at the start.

## 6. Who keeps it working after launch?

Software that nobody looks after drifts out of date. Ask who fixes problems, who makes changes as your business changes, and what that costs.

## A good sign

The best providers will tell you when something isn’t worth doing, or when a simpler tool would serve you better.
