Customers now get in touch wherever suits them: WhatsApp, Instagram, email, a website form, a phone call. Each of those has its own inbox, and none of them talk to each other. That’s how enquiries get lost, not through bad service but through nobody seeing them in time.

## Why enquiries slip

- **No single list.** If enquiries live in five places, nobody can see them all at once.
- **No owner.** When everyone can reply, it’s easy for everyone to assume someone else did.
- **No next step.** A reply is sent, the customer goes quiet, and there’s no reminder to check back.

## Three steps that fix most of it

**1. One list.** Every enquiry, from every channel, lands in the same place with the customer’s name, what they asked and where it came from. This can start as a shared spreadsheet; what matters is that it’s the only list.

**2. One owner per enquiry.** Each enquiry is assigned to a named person who is responsible for the reply and the follow-up. If they’re away, it’s reassigned, not left.

**3. A follow-up date, every time.** No enquiry is closed with “we’ll see”. It either becomes a sale, a clear no, or gets a date for the next check-in.

## Start with your busiest channel

Don’t try to connect everything on day one. Start with the channel that brings in the most enquiries, get the list, owner and follow-up habit working there, then add the next channel.

## Signs it’s working

- Enquiries get a reply the same working day.
- You can say, at any moment, how many enquiries are open and who owns them.
- Quiet customers get a follow-up without anyone having to remember.

> **Try this week**
>
> Pick your busiest channel and, for every enquiry, write down the owner and the follow-up date. At the end of the week, count how many follow-ups would otherwise have been missed.
