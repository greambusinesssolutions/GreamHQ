Should you buy something ready-made or have something built? It’s one of the most common questions growing businesses ask, and the honest answer is usually: start with what already exists.

## Why off-the-shelf usually wins first

- **It’s quicker.** You can often start this week.
- **It’s cheaper to run.** Updates, security and support are shared across many customers.
- **It’s proven.** Other businesses have already found the problems.

If a ready-made product does most of what you need, use it, and adjust a few habits to fit it.

## When custom earns its cost

Custom software makes sense when one of these is true:

- **Your team works around the tool every week,** copying data between apps, keeping side spreadsheets or doing the same steps by hand.
- **The way you make money is genuinely different,** so no product handles your pricing, process or customers properly.
- **The tool limits growth,** because it can’t handle your volume, locations or channels.

## A middle path

Often the best answer is both: off-the-shelf tools for the standard parts, connected together, with a small custom piece for the one part that’s unique to you. That keeps costs down while fixing what matters.

## Questions to ask yourself

1. What does the ready-made option not do that we need?
2. How many hours a week does that gap cost us?
3. Could we change how we work instead?
4. If we build, who keeps it working afterwards?

## Start small

If you do build, start with the one part that costs you the most and prove it’s worth it before building more.

> **Try this week**
>
> List the workarounds your team uses around your current tools. If they add up to several hours a week, it’s worth looking at what a better fit would save.
