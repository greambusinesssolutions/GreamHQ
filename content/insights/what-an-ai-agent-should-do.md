An AI agent is software that does one routine job for your team, in the channels you already use. Used well, it takes repetitive work off people’s plates. Used badly, it says the wrong thing to a customer at the worst moment. The difference is mostly in what you let it do.

## What an agent is good at

- **Answering common questions.** Opening hours, delivery days, how to book, what’s included.
- **Sending reminders.** Appointment reminders, follow-ups on quotes, renewal notices.
- **Checking and matching.** Confirming a payment arrived and matching it to the right order.
- **Summarising.** A short daily note of new enquiries, sales and what’s still owed.

These jobs have something in common: they happen often, the right answer is usually clear, and a person can easily check the result.

## What it shouldn’t do on its own

- **Set or change prices, or offer discounts.**
- **Handle complaints or sensitive situations.**
- **Make promises the business hasn’t agreed,** such as delivery dates or refunds.
- **Anything unusual.** If the request doesn’t fit a known pattern, it should go to a person.

## The safe setup

The safest arrangement is simple: the agent handles the routine, and a person approves anything that matters. In practice that means the agent drafts a reply for a bulk price, and someone on the team checks it before it goes out.

## Questions to settle before you start

- Which one job will the agent do first?
- What exactly does a person still approve?
- How will you know it’s getting things right?
- What happens when it doesn’t know the answer?

> **Try this week**
>
> List the five questions your team answers most often. If the answers rarely change, those are good candidates for an agent to draft, with a person checking.
