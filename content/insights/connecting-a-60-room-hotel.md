Hotels look complicated from the outside: bookings from several sites, rooms, a restaurant, extras, deposits and payments in different forms. When connecting one from booking to payment, the most useful lesson was where to begin.

## Start at the end: the bill

The tempting starting point is bookings, because that’s where the guest journey starts. But the problem owners feel most is at the other end: a guest checks out and something is still unpaid, or a restaurant bill never reaches their account.

So the first question was simple: *can the front desk see everything a guest owes before they leave?* Building backwards from that answer shaped every other decision.

## Every charge follows the guest

Room charges, restaurant bills, minibar, laundry and deposits all need to land on the same guest account, in the moment they happen, not at the end of the day. Once that’s true, the balance at check-out is simply correct.

## One step at a time, with the team using each part

Rather than switching everything on at once, each part was added only once the team was comfortable with the last: first the guest account and charges, then payments matched to it, then bookings from every channel flowing in.

## Small details matter

- **Clear statuses** at the front desk: *Paid*, *Balance due*, *Deposit only*.
- **One reference per stay** that every charge and payment uses.
- **A morning list** of guests checking out with a balance, so nobody is surprised at the desk.

## What we’d tell another hotel

Begin with the question your team asks most often at the desk, and make the answer instant. For most hotels, that question is “what does this guest owe?”

> **Try this week**
>
> For every guest checking out, note whether their final bill was complete before they reached the desk. Any gaps show where to start.
