For many businesses, checking a payment means scrolling: through bank alerts, through WhatsApp for a customer’s transfer screenshot, through a notebook of who said they’d pay on Friday. It works, slowly, until a payment is missed or a customer is chased for money they’ve already paid.

## Why it goes wrong

- **Payments and orders live in different places.** The bank knows the money arrived; the order book knows what was sold. Nobody connects them until later.
- **Screenshots aren’t confirmations.** A screenshot shows a transfer was started, not that it landed.
- **Part payments get lost.** “Paid 60%, balance Friday” is easy to forget by Friday.

## A simple system that works

**1. One list of orders.** Every order has a reference, the customer, the amount and a status.

**2. Clear statuses.** Keep it to a few: *Sent* (invoice or payment link sent), *Part paid*, *Paid*, *Overdue*. Every order is always in exactly one.

**3. Match as money arrives.** When a payment lands, find the order and update its status the same day. If the reference doesn’t match anything, flag it rather than guess.

**4. A daily glance.** Each morning, look at what’s overdue and what’s part paid. That’s your chase list.

## Make it easier to pay you

- Send a payment link or clear bank details with every invoice.
- Ask customers to use the order reference when they pay.
- Send a receipt the moment payment arrives, so they don’t chase you either.

## Signs it’s working

You can answer “has this customer paid?” in seconds, nobody is chased for money already received, and overdue balances are followed up the day they fall due.
