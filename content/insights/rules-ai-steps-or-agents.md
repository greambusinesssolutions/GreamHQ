When people talk about automating work, they often jump straight to AI. Many tasks don’t need it, and the ones that do don’t always need an agent. Choosing the simplest tool that does the job keeps things cheaper, faster and easier to trust.

## Rules: when the answer never changes

A rule is “when this happens, do that”. *When a payment arrives, send a receipt.* *When an enquiry is two days old with no reply, remind the owner.* Rules are fast, cheap and completely predictable. If a task can be written as a rule, use one.

## AI steps: when a task needs reading or writing

Some tasks involve language: reading a customer’s message to work out what they want, or drafting a reply. A single AI step can do that one part, inside an otherwise simple process. For example: read the enquiry, sort it into *price question*, *booking* or *complaint*, then hand it to the right person.

## Agents: when a job takes several steps

An agent handles a whole job that takes several steps and some judgement: reading an enquiry, checking availability, drafting a reply, logging it and setting a follow-up. That’s useful, but it needs clear limits. A person should approve anything that matters, such as prices, discounts and anything unusual.

## A quick way to decide

- **Same input, same answer every time?** Rule.
- **Needs to read or write a message?** AI step.
- **Several steps, some judgement, clear limits?** Agent, with a person approving what matters.

## Start simple

Most good systems are mostly rules, with AI used where it genuinely helps. If a rule will do, don’t reach for an agent.
