Most businesses have more work they could automate than they have time to set up. The question isn’t what’s possible; it’s what to do first. A good first job builds confidence. A bad one creates more work than it saves.

## The four-question test

**1. Is it frequent?** A job that happens every day pays back quickly. One that happens once a month rarely justifies the setup.

**2. Is it predictable?** If the right answer is usually clear, the work can be handed over. If every case needs judgement, it can’t, yet.

**3. Is it costly when missed?** The best first jobs are the ones where a slip loses a customer or money: an unanswered enquiry, a forgotten reminder, an unchased payment.

**4. Is it easy to check?** A person should be able to see at a glance whether it was done right. That’s what keeps you in control.

## Scoring it

Give each candidate job a yes or no for each question.

- **Four yeses:** a strong first job.
- **Three:** worth doing, with a person checking closely at first.
- **Two or fewer:** leave it with your team for now.

## Common first jobs that pass

- Sending a follow-up to every enquiry that hasn’t replied in two days.
- Reminding customers of appointments the day before.
- Confirming payments have arrived and flagging the ones that haven’t.

## Then measure

Before you switch anything on, note how the job is done today: how long it takes and how often it’s missed. After a few weeks, compare. If the numbers aren’t better, change the setup or stop.
